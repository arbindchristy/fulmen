# Pain And Differentiation

## Painful problem
- Enterprise negotiation knowledge is trapped in inboxes, scattered redlines, and a few senior negotiators’ heads.
- Teams repeatedly debate the same clauses and concessions without learning from prior outcomes.
- Approval bottlenecks happen because reviewers see static documents, not a clear explanation of tradeoffs and fallback positions.

## Why current tools are insufficient
- CLM products are optimized for storage, templates, and signatures, not live negotiation intelligence.
- Generic copilots can draft language, but they do not understand enterprise fallback rules, approval matrices, or commercial leverage.
- Shared docs and email threads do not preserve structured negotiation memory or decision quality.

## What the AI-native advantage is
- Agents can decompose a contract into issue clusters, propose fallback language, surface precedent, and model concession packages before a human commits.
- The product can maintain a negotiation graph showing clause dependencies, commercial impact, risk posture, and required approvals.
- Meeting prep, counterparty response analysis, and final approval packets become generated outputs of the workbench rather than manual prep work.

## Why this is not just “AI sprinkled on existing workflow”
- The product is not a template filler or chatbot on top of a CLM repository.
- The core interaction is a live deal room where strategy, redlining, precedent, and approval are unified.
- Without agentic clause reasoning and system-enforced negotiation policy, users still bounce across five tools and lose leverage.
