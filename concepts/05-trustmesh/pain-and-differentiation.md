# Pain And Differentiation

## Painful problem
- Enterprises spend heavily to identify supplier risk, then let the highest-value work degrade into manual remediation chasing.
- Suppliers submit inconsistent evidence, partial fixes, and generic narratives that are hard to review quickly and defensibly.
- Residual-risk exceptions are frequently approved with weak context because the case file is buried across email, portals, and spreadsheets.

## Why current tools are insufficient
- Most third-party risk tools are designed around questionnaires, scoring, and periodic reviews, not collaborative remediation execution.
- Ticketing systems and email can track tasks, but they do not normalize supplier evidence or preserve a clean assurance chain.
- Generic AI assistants can summarize a vendor response, but they do not enforce access boundaries between enterprise and supplier parties.

## What the AI-native advantage is
- Agents can interpret findings, draft remediation plans, review supplier submissions, and flag weak or non-responsive evidence.
- The product can detect repeated remediation patterns across the portfolio and suggest standard compensating controls or negotiation paths.
- Risk teams move from passive review to an active closure engine without pretending the model owns acceptance decisions.

## Why this is not just “AI sprinkled on existing workflow”
- The product is built around an ongoing, adversarial, multi-party remediation loop, not a one-time assessment or document summary.
- Without agentic evidence review, analysts still spend their time on the lowest-value reading and chasing work.
- Without system-enforced tenant isolation, approval, and audit, buyers will not trust a shared supplier workspace.
