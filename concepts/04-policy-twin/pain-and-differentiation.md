# Pain And Differentiation

## Painful problem
- New policies and regulations rarely fail at publication; they fail during translation into actual operational change.
- Teams spend weeks debating applicability, ownership, impacted systems, and evidence requirements because the source text is dense and responsibilities are fragmented.
- Leadership often gets a false sense of progress from attestation and status reports that are disconnected from real implementation.

## Why current tools are insufficient
- Policy management tools excel at storage, distribution, and attestation, not operational impact mapping.
- GRC tools can track controls after someone defines them, but they do not transform policy text into change packets with owners, deadlines, and approvals.
- Consulting-heavy approaches are expensive, slow, and impossible to repeat every time a policy changes.

## What the AI-native advantage is
- Agents can decompose policy text into obligations, map obligations to processes and systems, draft control deltas, and identify conflicts with existing policy or operating reality.
- The product can build a living policy twin that links source language to controls, systems, owners, evidence, and implementation state.
- Cross-functional teams get an operational translation layer instead of another document repository.

## Why this is not just “AI sprinkled on existing workflow”
- The core deliverable is an implementation-ready operating model for policy change, not a summary of the source document.
- Without agentic decomposition and mapping, the hardest work remains trapped in workshops and spreadsheets.
- Without system-enforced approvals and versioned traceability, the output is not trusted enough for policy-critical programs.
