# Pain And Differentiation

## Painful problem
- Enterprises already know which controls they are supposed to run; the real pain is proving they ran them correctly, on time, with enough evidence.
- Control evidence collection is manual, fragmented, and deadline-driven, which creates burnout, stale evidence, and weak audit defensibility.
- Audit findings often come less from missing controls than from missing proof, broken narratives, or inconsistent evidence chains.

## Why current tools are insufficient
- GRC suites are strong at inventories, control libraries, and attestations, but weak at actively collecting, reconciling, and packaging evidence.
- Ticketing and workflow tools can chase owners, but they do not understand whether the submitted artifact actually proves the control objective.
- Generic document AI can summarize artifacts, but it does not preserve source lineage, approval state, or control-specific acceptance criteria.

## What the AI-native advantage is
- Agents can assemble evidence from heterogeneous artifacts, detect contradictions, draft control narratives, and identify missing proof before a human reviewer wastes time.
- The product can maintain an evidence graph that links every assertion to source artifacts, collection timestamps, approvals, and policy state.
- Agents can proactively ask narrower follow-up questions instead of sending broad evidence requests that create rework.

## Why this is not just “AI sprinkled on existing workflow”
- The core job is not storage or ticket routing; it is turning messy operational exhaust into defensible control proof.
- Without agentic evidence interpretation, the user still does the hardest part manually.
- Without system-enforced provenance, approval, and audit logs, the output is not trusted enough to change buying behavior.
